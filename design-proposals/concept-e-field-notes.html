the letter

Dear Engineering Manager who can't take a week off,

You already know you should delegate. That's not the issue. The issue is that the work you carry contains context, judgment, relationships, and risk that can't simply be handed over.

So you keep carrying it. The approvals. The escalations. The "quick questions." The roadmap that changes every week. The AI output that still needs your judgment.

Your competence has become the organization's dependency. That's not a character flaw — it's a structural problem. And structural problems have structural fixes.

The Push is how we fix it: not by asking you to work harder or "let go," but by redesigning how decisions, ownership, and judgment move through your team — inside your real work, not in a workshop.

— Itay

the method, in five notes
note 01

Diagnose the Dependency

Find exactly where you became the default route for progress. We call it the Player Trap — still playing the role that made you successful.

↳ goal: make it visible, no blame
note 02

Expose the Invisible Executor

The connecting, translating, reviewing, calming — work no role description contains. Invisible work can't be transferred until it's written down.

↳ goal: a real map of the load
note 03

Clarify Decision Rights

Which decisions the team owns, which need consultation, which escalate. Ambiguity removed, control kept where it matters.

↳ goal: less approval-seeking
note 04

Transfer Judgment, Not Tasks

Principles, trade-offs, feedback on judgment. You stop being the answer and start improving how answers get produced.

↳ goal: decision capacity in the team
note 05

Build Strategic Capacity

Direction, org design, architecture, AI governance — the work only you can do. This is what leadership time is actually for.

↳ goal: higher-leverage leadership
