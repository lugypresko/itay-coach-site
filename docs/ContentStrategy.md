# Content Strategy

## Purpose

This document defines how the Authority Engine should select, create, govern, publish, and measure content.

The goal is not to maximize publishing volume.

The goal is to create a coherent body of evidence-backed content that establishes Itay Foyerstein as an authority on technology leadership, operating models, predictable execution, and AI-enabled organizational transformation — and converts the right readers into qualified conversations.

---

## Business Outcome

The Authority Engine exists to generate:

> Qualified conversations with technology leaders and organizations that can become paying clients, consulting engagements, coaching programs, strategic roles, or long-term partnerships.

The operating chain is:

```text
Source Knowledge
→ Approved Insight
→ Reader-Facing Artifact
→ Governed Publication
→ Discovery
→ Authority
→ Trust
→ CTA Engagement
→ Qualified Conversation
→ Commercial Outcome
```

Publishing is not the outcome.

Traffic is not the outcome.

The outcome is qualified demand created through trusted expertise.

Content should consistently connect to one or more of these problems:

1. The leader is the bottleneck.
2. Execution depends on a few strong people.
3. Product, engineering, and business priorities are misaligned.
4. Managers remain executors instead of building systems.
5. Decision rights are unclear.
6. The roadmap changes faster than the organization can absorb.
7. AI increases output but not business impact.
8. Cross-functional friction creates delay and rework.

The Authority Engine should reinforce one central proposition:

> Technology execution becomes predictable when leaders stop compensating for the system and start redesigning it.

## Content Governance

Every governed artifact must include:

- artifact ID
- artifact type
- version
- deterministic content hash
- source references
- provenance record
- semantic review
- approval state
- publication state
- proposed and final route
- publication timestamp
- verification evidence

Semantic review, provenance, approval, and publication must reference the same:

```text
artifactId + version + contentHash
```

No artifact may be published when these references diverge.

5. Reader-facing completeness  
6. Differentiation  
7. Claim support  
8. Brand-voice alignment  
9. CTA relevance  
10. Internal-language exposure

## PageBrief Boundary

PageBrief is an internal planning artifact.

It may contain:

- target audience
- search intent
- page proposition
- content requirements
- proof requirements
- internal links
- CTA strategy
- differentiation notes

PageBrief must never be rendered directly as public content.

A reader-facing artifact must contain complete prose independently of the PageBrief.

Public rendering must fail closed if only internal planning structures are available.

---

## Publication Invariants

- HTTP 200 is not proof of successful publication.
- A route with empty content is not published successfully.
- A passing build is not production completion.
- A deployment marked READY is not proof of correct content.
- Repository SHA must match deployed SHA where required.
- Live rendered output must match the approved artifact.
- Sitemap, canonical, robots, metadata, and `llms.txt` must reflect the same publication decision.
- Indexing must remain disabled until launch readiness is approved.
- No human approval may be inferred or bypassed.
- No migration may run without explicit authorization.

## Editorial Prioritization

Score content opportunities against:

- strategic relevance
- audience pain intensity
- commercial proximity
- differentiation
- source-evidence strength
- internal-link value
- search demand
- production readiness
- effort
- duplication risk

Recommended priority formula:

```text
Priority =
Strategic Relevance
× Pain Intensity
× Evidence Strength
× Commercial Proximity
÷ Effort and Risk
```

Do not prioritize only by search volume.

---

## Publishing Cadence

Do not commit to a fixed content quota until the publishing pipeline is stable.

Recommended operating cadence after launch:

- one substantial authority artifact every 2–4 weeks
- supporting posts derived from the same evidence
- periodic case-study or evidence updates
- quarterly content-pruning and consolidation
- continuous internal-link and conversion review

One strong evidence-backed asset is more valuable than five generic posts.

## Final Content Decision Test

Before creating or publishing any artifact, ask:

1. Which audience problem does this address?
2. Which strategic pillar does it strengthen?
3. What source evidence supports it?
4. What distinct role does it play?
5. What should the reader understand or do afterward?
6. Does existing content already serve this intent?
7. Is the artifact reader-facing rather than an internal plan?
8. Is every material claim supported?
9. Is it bound to the correct identity, version, and hash?
10. Will it move the Authority Engine toward qualified commercial outcomes?

If the answer is unclear, do not publish yet.
