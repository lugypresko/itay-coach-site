### About Itay Foyerstein

#### Who is Itay Foyerstein?

Itay Foyerstein is a technology leadership advisor and coach with more than 25 years of experience across engineering, product, delivery, transformation, and cross-functional technology leadership. He has worked inside complex technology organizations and with leaders responsible for teams, execution, organizational change, and strategic programs.

His work focuses on the point where strong technical leaders become too central to execution: too many decisions reach them, too much knowledge sits with them, and the team cannot move without their involvement.

The Push was built from that operating reality, not from generic coaching theory.

#### Why did Itay create The Push?

Itay created The Push after repeatedly seeing the same pattern: capable technology leaders were promoted because they were excellent at solving problems, but the same strength later made them the bottleneck.

They continued carrying decisions, escalations, technical context, and delivery pressure long after their role required them to build ownership in others.

The Push was designed to help leaders make that transition deliberately—from being the person who keeps everything moving to building a leadership system that can move without their constant intervention.

#### What experience does Itay bring to the process?

Itay brings experience from more than two decades of technology leadership, consulting, program delivery, product operations, organizational transformation, and management development.

He has worked with engineering and business leaders on problems such as delivery delays, cross-functional execution, decision-making, leadership transitions, stakeholder alignment, team ownership, and operating-model redesign.

That experience allows him to combine coaching with practical advisory. He can challenge how you think while also understanding the organizational, technical, and commercial environment in which you have to act.

#### Why work with Itay instead of a general executive coach?

A general executive coach may help you reflect, clarify goals, and examine your behavior. Itay does that, but he also understands how engineering organizations actually operate.

He can work with the reality behind the leadership issue: architecture decisions, delivery pressure, cross-functional dependencies, unclear ownership, technical credibility, stakeholder politics, and teams that escalate every difficult problem upward.

The difference is not that you receive more advice. It is that the coaching is grounded in the operating environment of technology leadership and converted into changes you can test in your real work.
